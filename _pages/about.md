Hello 👋! I'm Stefanos, a first-year PhD student in the Department of Statistical Science at University College London (UCL) and part of the [Fundamentals of Statistical Machine Learning](https://fsml-ucl.github.io/) group. I'm supervised by [François-Xavier Briol](https://fxbriol.github.io/) and [Alessandro Barp](https://alebarp.github.io/), and my PhD is co-funded by UCL's EPSRC CDT in [Collaborative Computational Modelling at the Interface](https://ccmi-cdt.org/) and the Department of Statistical Science.

My research is on simulation-based inference and interpretable AI. I'm broadly interested in Bayesian approaches to machine learning, and in making the methods we use to speed up science more reliable even when data is scarce. Recently, I've become interested in approaching these problems through a geometric lens.

# Background

Before my PhD, I spent a year and a half as a Research Assistant at Cambridge with Zoe Kourtzi and Peter Tiňo, working on the early prognosis of dementia. I built ODE-based models of how the disease progresses in individual patients, along with interpretable prototype-based classifiers that learn which features matter most. Our team's entry won second place in the prognostic markers track of the 2026 Global Neurodegeneration Proteomics Consortium (GNPC) Data Challenge. Before that, I spent nine months as a Research Assistant at Sheffield with Eleni Vasilaki, where I worked on Neural ODE and SDE models for reinforcement learning ([TMLR](https://openreview.net/forum?id=T6OrPlyPV4)).

I hold an MSc in Advanced Computing from Imperial College London. For my thesis, supervised by Pedro Mediano, I used graph neural network autoencoders to reconstruct brain activity from MEG. Before that, I completed a BSc in Computer Science at King's College London. My undergraduate thesis, with Andrew King, Hanna Chockler and Alex Hammers, studied demographic bias in brain MRI segmentation ([MLCN](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-17899-3_2)).
